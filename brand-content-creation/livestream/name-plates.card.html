A stream is watched in the middle. Somebody arriving at minute forty sees a face and hears an opinion, and the name plate is the only thing on the frame that tells them whose opinion it is and why it is worth anything. Three levels in one object: the icon says a person, the heading weight says who, and the mono line says why they are on this frame.

Rules

POSITIONBottom-left, inside the 90% title-safe boxAt 96, 878 on a 1080p canvas, about 700 × 86. Left, because the bottom-right corner belongs to the camera, the mark and the toast lane.
DURATION8s host, 10s guest, persistent only for N5One appearance per person per scene entry. If somebody has been on screen for twenty minutes, the plate has already done its job — bring it back when they return, not on a timer.
MOTIONSlides in from the left edge, 240ms, --ease-outLeaves the same way it arrived. No fade, no scale, no typewriter effect on the name.
NAMEAs the person writes it, in heading weightAsk a guest how their name is spelled and how it is said, before the stream. Getting it wrong on screen is worse than not showing it at all.
JOB TITLESix words maximum, mono, no company logo“Flow Architect”, not “Senior Manager, Platform Engineering (Automation)”. If the title will not fit, it is a title the audience does not need.
ICONRole, not decorationuser-circle host · users-three guest · microphone speaking · projector-screen driving · seal-check credential. Five glyphs, five meanings, no others.

Do

  • Show every plate on a frame at once, or none. One named person beside an unnamed one ranks them.
  • Bring the host plate back after every long absence — a return from screen-only is a new arrival for half the room.
  • Keep the same plate in the VOD. It is the frame a clip gets cropped to, and the only attribution that travels.

Never

  • A permanent name plate. After eight seconds it has stopped being information and started being a logo.
  • A company logo inside the plate. The mark in the corner is the only logo the frame carries.
  • Two plates in different sizes on one frame. Use N3 for both, or N1 for both.
