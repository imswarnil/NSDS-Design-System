CANVAS
1920 × 1080, 16:9, alwaysEvery scene, every source, every export. 1280×720 only if upload is the constraint — never a non-16:9 canvas.
TITLE-SAFE
Inner 90% — all text lives hereThe solid brand rule. Nothing readable crosses it: platform chrome, chat panels and phone notches eat the outer band.
ACTION-SAFE
Inner 93% — dashedGraphics may touch it; only full-bleed backgrounds and the top accent bar go past.
GRID
12 columns × 8 rowsEvery zone snaps to it. A panel that starts at a non-column edge is the first sign a scene was improvised.
01 KICKER
Top-left, mono, uppercase// LIVE SESSION 04 — the same eyebrow every thumbnail uses. Names the series, never the mood.
02 STAGE
The teaching surfaceScreen, slides or camera. It is the only zone allowed to change between scenes; everything else stays nailed down.
03 LIVE
Top-right, error red, whole broadcastOff on the countdown and the ending — the badge means you are talking, not that the encoder is running.
04 CAM
Bottom-right gutter, 17–23.5% widthSame corner all stream. The ratio is chosen per layout — 16:9, 4:3, 1:1 or 9:16, whichever fills the block without dead space — but never two ratios in one session.
05 NAME PLATE
Bottom-left, 8s, icon + name + job titleThe only graphic that answers “who is this” for somebody who joined at minute forty. One per person, per appearance.
06 HANDLE
Bottom-left, rotates every ~10 minOne destination at a time. Shares its zone with the chip lane — never both at once.
07 MARK
Bottom-right, 55–65% opacity, always onThe frame's signature, not an element of the layout. Never animated after the intro.
08 RAIL
Right column, 23.5%, comment scenes onlyIt takes width from the stage rather than covering it. Four rows maximum, newest at the bottom, one promoted at a time.
09 CHIPS
Bottom-left, above the handleSubscribe, like, bell, newsletter, visit. Two chips maximum, one of them filled — and never during a demo.
10 TOASTS
Bottom-right, above the cam, one at a timeSubscriber, member, tip, milestone, link, technical notice. They queue rather than stack, and they never cover the mark.
