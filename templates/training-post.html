Recalculation is asynchronous

Role hierarchy grants access upward, and that is the part everyone remembers. The part that causes the incident is what happens when a record moves.

Video + notes Article 11 min Post 2 of 6 Intermediate Read
11:04 Training

Captions Transcript below Read instead

When a record is reparented, its sharing rows are recalculated in the background — and until that finishes, the old rows are still live. This post is about the window in between, because that window is where the incident lives.

What actually happens on reparent

Sharing in Salesforce is materialized, not computed on read: the share tables hold a row for every grant, and a query checks rows, not rules. That design is why record access is fast at scale — and it has a consequence. When you change the thing the rows were derived from, the platform has to rebuild them, and it does that in a queue, not in your transaction.

So the sequence on a reparent is:

  • your DML commits — the record's owner or parent has changed;
  • a recalculation job is enqueued, behind every other recalculation in the org;
  • until it runs, the old share rows answer every access check;
  • when it finishes, the new rows do.
The window In a quiet org the queue drains in seconds. In an org mid data-migration it can be hours — and a report run in that window is correct according to the database and wrong according to your compliance team.

Seeing it in Apex

You can watch the window from code. Query the share table immediately after the reparent and the old rows are still there:

Nothing in that query is wrong. The rows are real; they are simply the rows of a world that no longer exists. That distinction — stale but valid — is why no exception fires anywhere.

What triggers a recalculation

Not every edit does. The queue only takes jobs from changes that alter derived sharing:

ChangeRecalculates?Why
Record owner changesYesOwner-based rules and the hierarchy both derive from it
Record is reparentedYesImplicit parent access derives from the lookup
A role moves in the hierarchyYes — org-wideEvery grant through that branch is derived
A field on the record changesNoField values are not in the share tables
Manual share addedNoA manual share is a row — nothing to derive

The third row is the one that bites: moving a role near the top of the hierarchy recalculates every branch beneath it. That is why the platform sometimes defers those jobs to off-peak hours — and why your “quick org chart tidy-up” on a Friday afternoon is not quick.

In short

Never read sharing state in the same transaction that changed its inputs. If a process depends on post-recalculation access, chain it — platform event, queueable, or simply the next user action — and treat the share tables as eventually consistent, because they are.
