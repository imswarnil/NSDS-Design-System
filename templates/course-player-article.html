Aggregate queries, and when to stop using them

18 min readupdated Jan 2026

SOQL can count, sum and group for you. That is convenient right up to the row where it stops being allowed, and knowing where that row is saves a rewrite.

What an aggregate query is

An aggregate query asks the database for a summary rather than for records: how many opportunities per stage, the total amount per owner, the newest case per account. The platform does the arithmetic before the rows ever reach your Apex, which is why it is faster than looping and why it counts differently against the limits.

Where it stops

An aggregate query still returns rows, and those rows still count: 50,000 of them, same as any other query. A GROUP BY on a field with high cardinality — a date, an id, an email — will hit that ceiling on a real org, and it will do it in production rather than in your scratch org.

Watch outGrouping by a date field groups by the timestamp, not the day. Use DAY_ONLY() or you get one row per second.

What to reach for instead

  • A rollup summary field, when the relationship is master-detail and the number is wanted on the record.
  • A scheduled batch writing to a summary object, when the grouping is expensive and the answer can be an hour old.
  • A report type, when a human is the consumer and nobody needs it in Apex at all.
