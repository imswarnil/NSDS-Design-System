The data model is the product

Every automation problem I have been called in to fix was a schema problem wearing a costume. Here is how to tell the difference before you write a line of Apex.

Swarnil Singhai 28 July 2026 12 min read
Illustration · Namaste Salesforce

The request is always the same shape. Something in the org is slow, or wrong, or both, and the automation is blamed. Nine times out of ten the automation is doing exactly what it was told, against a schema that was never designed to be asked the question.

The costume

A flow that runs for ninety seconds is not a flow problem. It is a query returning forty thousand rows because the object it filters on has no selective field, and the person who added the field three years ago had no reason to think anyone would filter on it.

The tell is that the fix keeps moving. You optimise the flow and the batch job slows down. You fix the batch job and the report times out. Each fix is real and none of them holds, because the thing they have in common is underneath all three.

If two fixes in a row move the problem rather than removing it, stop fixing and go and look at the schema.

What a good model buys

A schema that matches the questions people actually ask makes the automation boring. The query is selective because the filter is on a field that means something. The rollup is cheap because the relationship is the one the business already has. Nothing is clever, and nothing needs to be.

TipBefore adding a field, write down the query it exists to make possible. If you cannot write the query, you do not need the field yet.

The uncomfortable part

Changing a data model in a live org is expensive and slow and everybody knows it, which is exactly why the costume keeps working. It is always cheaper this quarter to add another automation than to admit the shape is wrong.

The honest thing is to say so out loud, in the review, with the number attached: this fix costs a day and buys six months; the model change costs three weeks and buys the rest of the org’s life. Then let somebody decide with the real figures in front of them.
